## 6. Why N8 remains pending

This distinction is essential to the research objective.

The reading brain is not simply a visual character classifier. Research describes a hierarchical, distributed network involving early visual, higher-level visual and language-related regions. The VWFA acts as an important mediator in that network. <Cite refs={["turn989636search5","turn989636search4"]}/>

Predictive coding provides a useful research hypothesis: orthographic expectations may explain away predictable visual input, leaving informative prediction errors for subsequent processing. <Cite ref="turn989636search1"/>

The current ORE extension implements simplified computational versions of some of these ideas. It does **not** yet reproduce the actual cortical architecture or demonstrate that its calculations match human neural activity.

N8 therefore requires real, rights-cleared Latin and Arabic image corpora, genuinely independent font/writer splits, external OCR baselines, multiple seeds, and ideally behavioral or neural measurements. The roadmap specifies the experiments and acceptance criteria.

**My final assessment:** The ORE software now has an independently inspectable neurocomputational research layer, integrated with the accepted A–J system. The development work through N7 is complete. The remaining question is no longer whether the software modules execute, but whether their proposed mechanisms explain real human reading behavior and neural evidence.
